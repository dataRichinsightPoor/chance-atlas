Where the randomness enters,
with the assumptions showing.

Version 0.1.0-alpha. This page states the model behind each calculator in the atlas, the assumptions it rests on, and the test that checks the implementation. Every derivation is standard; the atlas's contribution is to put the closed forms where an experiment is being designed. Symbols follow the catalog entries. All numbers on the atlas are synthetic forward-model output.

Conventions

\(\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda)\) has mass \(e^{-\lambda}\lambda^k/k!\). \(\mathrm{Binomial}(n,p)\) has mass \(\binom{n}{k}p^k(1-p)^{n-k}\). \(\Phi\) is the standard normal distribution function and \(\varphi\) its density. The negative binomial is parameterized by burst frequency \(r\) and burst size \(b\), with mass \(\frac{\Gamma(k+r)}{k!\,\Gamma(r)}\left(\frac{1}{1+b}\right)^r\left(\frac{b}{1+b}\right)^k\). Special functions are implemented in models.js with a Lanczos log-gamma (relative error below \(10^{-13}\)), a rational erf approximation (absolute error below \(1.2\times10^{-7}\)), Acklam's inverse normal with one Newton step, and a 400-point trapezoid rule for the bivariate normal.

Poisson loading of partitions

Units land in partitions independently at mean \(\lambda\), so counts are Poisson. The empty fraction gives \(\lambda = -\ln f_0\) without knowledge of the seeding count, because thinning a Poisson process by any per-unit survival probability leaves it Poisson.

\[ P(k \ge 2 \mid k \ge 1) = 1 - \frac{\lambda e^{-\lambda}}{1 - e^{-\lambda}} \]

If each unit passes a screen with probability \(\phi\) independently, a partition passes with probability \(1-(1-\phi)^k\). Passing units form a thinned Poisson process with mean \(\phi\lambda\), so \(P(\text{exactly one unit} \mid \text{pass}) = \phi\lambda e^{-\lambda}/(1-e^{-\phi\lambda})\). As \(\phi \to 0\) this tends to \(e^{-\lambda}\), the size-biased result of Patil and Rao: a partition is drawn in proportion to its count, and the remaining count is again \(\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda)\). At low \(\lambda\) the random risk is \(\lambda/2\) and the selected risk is \(\lambda\), so the ratio tends to 2. Test: values at \(f_0 = 0.7\) against Clonality Check, and the limit ratio at \(f_0 = 0.999\).

Multiplicity of infection and transduction

Functional copies per cell are \(\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda)\) with \(\lambda = m f_{\mathrm{inf}}\). Among transduced cells, \(P(k \ge 2) = (1 - e^{-\lambda} - \lambda e^{-\lambda})/(1 - e^{-\lambda})\). If each copy produces a scoring phenotype independently with probability \(\phi\), a cell scores with probability \(1-(1-\phi)^k\), and the copy distribution among scoring cells is \(P(k)\,(1-(1-\phi)^k)/\sum_j P(j)(1-(1-\phi)^j)\). For small \(\phi\) this is the size-biased distribution, whose mean is \(\lambda + 1\). Test: pmf normalization and the ordering of multi-copy shares.

Endpoint-dilution titration

With \(n\) wells at each of a geometric series of dilutions with factor \(d\), the expected positive fraction at dose \(x\) is \(p = 1 - e^{-x}\) (one infectious unit suffices). The Spearman–Kärber estimator of the log titer has variance \((\log_{10} d)^2 \sum_i p_i(1-p_i)/(n-1)\), summed over dilution levels. The calculator evaluates this at the expected \(p_i\) for a given true titer. Test: the standard error scales as \(1/\sqrt{n-1}\) and increases with \(d\) at fixed range.

Low-copy detection limits

The number of target copies in a reaction is \(\mathrm{Poisson}(c)\), with \(c\) the mean copies after extraction and partitioning losses. \(P(\text{at least one copy}) = 1 - e^{-c}\), so a 95 percent detection rate requires \(c \ge -\ln 0.05 = 2.996\) copies before any amplification inefficiency. With \(r\) replicates, \(P(\text{all positive}) = (1-e^{-c})^r\) and \(P(\text{any positive}) = 1 - e^{-cr}\). Test: closed-form values.

Cell seeding and event counting

A count of \(N\) independent events has Poisson variance \(N\), so the coefficient of variation is \(1/\sqrt{N}\) and the events needed for a target CV are \(1/\mathrm{CV}^2\). The approximate 95 percent interval \(N \pm 1.96\sqrt{N}\) is adequate above about 30 events; below that the Clopper–Pearson interval should be used. Test: closed form.

Library representation and bottlenecks

At each bottleneck the count of a neutral element is resampled as Poisson around its current count (the library is held at fixed coverage, so the expected count is unchanged). Composing \(k\) such stages from an initial count \(c\) gives a Galton–Watson process with Poisson offspring of mean 1; the variance after \(k\) stages is \(kc\), so the CV is \(\sqrt{k/c}\). The calculator iterates the exact distribution on a truncated support and reports the probabilities of falling below or above fold-change thresholds, and of loss. Test: CV against \(\sqrt{k/c}\) and one-stage loss \(e^{-c}\).

Sequencing and mass spectrometry undersampling

If reads fall on molecules at random, reads per molecule are \(\mathrm{Poisson}(m)\) and the fraction of molecules seen is \(1 - e^{-m}\), the Lander–Waterman result. The duplicate fraction among reads is \(1 - (1-e^{-m})/m\). A clonotype of frequency \(f\) is seen in \(n\) reads with probability \(1-(1-f)^n \approx 1 - e^{-fn}\). Test: closed forms.

PCR as a branching process

Each molecule is copied in a cycle with probability \(e\), giving offspring 2 with probability \(e\) and 1 otherwise: mean \(1+e\), variance \(e(1-e)\). For a Galton–Watson process with offspring mean \(\mu\) and variance \(\sigma^2\) starting from one individual, \(\mathrm{Var}(Z_n) = \sigma^2\mu^{n-1}(\mu^n-1)/(\mu-1)\). Substituting,

\[ \mathrm{CV}^2(Z_n) = \frac{1-e}{1+e}\left(1 - (1+e)^{-n}\right) \]

which tends to \((1-e)/(1+e)\) after a few cycles. Independent templates add, so \(N_0\) templates divide the \(\mathrm{CV}^2\) by \(N_0\). With \(U\) equiprobable identifiers and \(N\) molecules, the expected number of distinct identifiers is \(U(1-e^{-N/U})\); the collision fraction is one minus this over \(N\). Test: simulation of 4,000 Galton–Watson realizations at \(e = 0.7\), \(n = 12\), and the UMI closed form.

Allele dropout and preferential amplification

Two templates are each captured and amplified with probability \(q\) independently. \(P(\text{both}) = q^2\), \(P(\text{exactly one}) = 2q(1-q)\), \(P(\text{neither}) = (1-q)^2\). Among samples that amplify, the dropout fraction is \(2q(1-q)/(1-(1-q)^2) = 2(1-q)/(2-q)\). Test: closed form.

Capture and dropout in single-cell sequencing

Given \(n\) true molecules, the observed count is \(\mathrm{Binomial}(n,p)\) and \(P(\text{zero}) = (1-p)^n\). For a true count with mean \(\bar n\) and coefficient of variation \(\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{true}}\), binomial thinning gives \(\mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{obs}} = p^2\mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{true}} + p(1-p)\bar n\), so \(\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{obs}}^2 = \mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{true}}^2 + (1-p)/(p\bar n)\). The technical share is the second term divided by the sum. Test: closed form.

Photon shot noise and camera noise

Detected photons are \(\mathrm{Poisson}(N)\); read noise adds variance \(\sigma_r^2\). \(\mathrm{SNR} = N/\sqrt{N+\sigma_r^2}\). The probability that a measurement falls below a threshold \(T\) is the Poisson distribution function at \(\lfloor T\rfloor\) (read noise ignored for this tail). Test: SNR at \(N = 100\) with \(\sigma_r = 0\).

Photobleaching and blinking

If each of \(n\) subunits carries a visible fluorophore with probability \(p\) (maturation times labeling times detection), the observed step count is \(\mathrm{Binomial}(n,p)\). The probability of observing all \(n\) steps is \(p^n\); the mode is \(\lfloor (n+1)p\rfloor\) with ties possible. The estimator for \(p\) from the mean step count \(m\) is \(m/n\). Test: normalization, \(p^n\), and the mode at \(n = 4\), \(p = 0.75\).

Number fluctuations in small volumes

Molecules in a volume \(V\) at concentration \(c\) number \(\mathrm{Poisson}(\langle N\rangle)\) with \(\langle N\rangle = cVN_A\). The relative variance is \(1/\langle N\rangle\), which is the zero-lag amplitude of the fluorescence autocorrelation function. Test: \(\langle N\rangle = 0.602\) at 1 nM in 1 fL.

The physical limit of concentration sensing

A sphere of radius \(a\) in concentration \(c\) receives molecules by diffusion at rate \(4\pi Dac\). A perfect absorber counts \(4\pi DacT\) independent Poisson arrivals in time \(T\), so its relative error is \(1/\sqrt{4\pi DacT}\). Berg and Purcell's perfectly monitoring sphere binds and releases every molecule that reaches it, and a released molecule is far more likely than a fresh one to return and be counted again; the correlated recounting raises the variance to \(\langle(\delta c)^2\rangle/c^2 = 3/(5\pi DacT)\), a factor \(12/5\) above the absorber. The calculator reports both. Test: scaling with \(T\) and the \(12/5\) variance ratio.

Stochastic ion channel gating

\(N\) identical channels each open with probability \(p_o\) give \(\mathrm{Binomial}(N,p_o)\) open channels. With unitary current \(i\), the mean current is \(I = Nip_o\) and the variance is \(i^2Np_o(1-p_o) = iI - I^2/N\), a parabola in \(I\) with apex at \(p_o = 1/2\). Fitting the parabola recovers \(i\) and \(N\) (non-stationary noise analysis). Test: variance at \(p_o = 1/2\) and the apex location.

Transcriptional bursting and expression noise

In the bursty limit of the two-state model, mRNA count is negative binomial with \(r\) the burst frequency per mRNA lifetime and \(b\) the burst size: mean \(rb\), variance \(rb(1+b)\), Fano factor \(1+b\). The fraction of cells above a threshold \(T\) is one minus the distribution function at \(T-1\). Doubling \(r\) doubles the mean with unchanged Fano factor; doubling \(b\) doubles the mean and nearly doubles the Fano factor. Test: normalization, mean and Fano factor of the implemented pmf.

Variability and memory of protein levels

Let log protein level be a stationary Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process with unit variance and mixing time \(\tau\). Sorting the top fraction \(q\) selects \(Z > z_q\) with \(z_q = \Phi^{-1}(1-q)\); the conditional mean is \(\varphi(z_q)/q\) and the conditional variance \(1 + z_q\varphi(z_q)/q - (\varphi(z_q)/q)^2\). After time \(t\) the mean excess decays by \(e^{-t/\tau}\) and the variance becomes \(\mathrm{Var}_0 e^{-2t/\tau} + (1-e^{-2t/\tau})\). On the linear scale with log standard deviation \(\sigma\), the fold enrichment is \(\exp(\sigma m_t + \sigma^2 v_t/2)/\exp(\sigma^2/2)\). Test: truncated-normal mean at \(q = 0.05\) and the decay factor.

Fractional killing and survivor enrichment

A protective state \(x = \exp(\sigma Z)\) with \(Z\) standard normal; death occurs when the dose exceeds the state's capacity, so survival of dose \(D\) is \(P(Z > z_c) = 1-\Phi(z_c)\) with \(z_c = \ln(D/D_{50})/\sigma\). If the state at a second dose has correlation \(\rho\) with the state at the first, the pair \((Z_1,Z_2)\) is bivariate normal and

\[ P(\text{survive both}) = 1 - 2\Phi(z_c) + \Phi_2(z_c, z_c; \rho) \]

The conditional survival of survivors is this divided by \(1-\Phi(z_c)\), which exceeds first-dose survival for any \(\rho > 0\). Test: simulation of 60,000 correlated pairs at \(\sigma = 1\), \(D/D_{50} = 3\), \(\rho = 0.8\).

Phenotypic switching and persisters

Two subpopulations with fractions \(1-f\) and \(f\) die at rates \(k_s\) and \(k_p\). The surviving fraction is \((1-f)e^{-k_s t} + fe^{-k_p t}\). The time to 99.9 percent kill is found by bisection and compared with \(\ln(1000)/k_s\). Switching during exposure is neglected, which is appropriate when exposure is short compared with switching times. Test: closed form and ordering.

Epigenetic switching, silencing and monoallelic expression

A two-state Markov chain with rates \(k_{\mathrm{off}}\) (expressing to silent) and \(k_{\mathrm{on}}\) (silent to expressing) per generation has expressing fraction \(p(t) = p_{\mathrm{eq}} + (p_0 - p_{\mathrm{eq}})e^{-(k_{\mathrm{on}}+k_{\mathrm{off}})t}\) with \(p_{\mathrm{eq}} = k_{\mathrm{on}}/(k_{\mathrm{on}}+k_{\mathrm{off}})\). Test: the asymptote.

Growth and cell-cycle stochasticity

By the renewal central limit theorem, the number of divisions completed in a fixed time has mean \(\bar n\) and variance \(\bar n\,\mathrm{CV}_T^2\), where \(\mathrm{CV}_T\) is the coefficient of variation of the division time. Clone size is \(2^{\text{divisions}}\), so log clone size has standard deviation \(\sigma_{\ln} = \ln 2\cdot\mathrm{CV}_T\sqrt{\bar n}\) and the size is approximately lognormal. The 90th-to-10th percentile ratio is \(\exp(2\times1.2816\,\sigma_{\ln})\). Test: closed form.

Mutation timing and jackpots

Mutations arise during the exponential expansion of a culture at rate \(\mu\) per division; with \(m = \mu N\) expected mutations, the number of mutant cells at the end follows the Lea–Coulson distribution, whose generating function is \((1-z)^{m(1-z)/z}\) and whose probabilities follow the Ma–Sandri–Sarkar recursion

\[ p_0 = e^{-m}, \qquad p_r = \frac{m}{r}\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\frac{p_i}{r-i+1} \]

The distribution has infinite mean in the idealized model. The zero-class estimator is \(m = -\ln(\text{fraction of cultures with no mutants})\). Test: \(p_0\), \(p_1 = me^{-m}/2\), truncated mass, and the tail against Poisson.

Clonal drift and evolution of cell lines

A subclone at frequency \(p\) with selective advantage \(s\) per passage moves to \(p' = pe^s/(pe^s + 1 - p)\), and drift at an effective population size \(N\) adds variance \(p'(1-p')/N\). The calculator propagates the mean and a first-order variance across passages. Without selection the variance after \(k\) passages is \(p(1-p)(1-(1-1/N)^k)\). Test: variance against that closed form at \(s = 0\).

Mitochondrial heteroplasmy segregation

Random sampling of \(n\) segregating units from heteroplasmy \(h\) gives variance \(h(1-h)/n\) in the next generation and \(h(1-h)(1-(1-1/n)^g)\) after \(g\) generations of drift. The calculator uses a normal approximation for threshold probabilities. Test: closed form.

Initiating-cell frequency by limiting-dilution transplantation

Under the single-hit model, \(P(\text{take at dose } x) = 1 - e^{-xf}\). The log-likelihood in \(\theta = \ln f\) has Fisher information \(\sum_i n_i (x_i f)^2 e^{-x_i f}/(1-e^{-x_i f})\), and the standard error of \(\ln f\) is its inverse square root. The information per animal peaks near \(xf \approx 1.6\) (take rate about 80 percent) and vanishes at doses that always or never take. Test: the standard error halves with four times the replicates.

Replicate hierarchy and hidden covariates

For wells nested in plates nested in days, the variance of the condition mean is \(\sigma_{\mathrm{day}}^2/n_d + \sigma_{\mathrm{plate}}^2/(n_d n_p) + \sigma_{\mathrm{well}}^2/(n_d n_p n_w)\). Treating all wells as independent gives \((\sigma_{\mathrm{day}}^2 + \sigma_{\mathrm{plate}}^2 + \sigma_{\mathrm{well}}^2)/(n_d n_p n_w)\), which is smaller whenever the higher-level variances are nonzero. Test: shares sum to one and the true standard error exceeds the naive one.

The hooked penetration curve

Primary stage. In the binding-site-barrier regime (\(C_0\) much smaller than the antigen density \(B_T\)), antibody saturates antigen as it advances and the front depth after time \(t\) is \(L = \sqrt{2DtC_0/B_T}\), the shrinking-core result used by Thurber, Schmidt and Wittrup, capped at the radius \(R\). Occupancy within the stained shell is \(\theta = C_0/(C_0+K_d)\).

Detection stage. The number of secondaries per bound primary is \(n_s = n_{\max}(1-\gamma\theta)\), a linear crowding law in which \(\gamma\) is the fractional loss at saturation. Dye density is \(\rho = B_T\theta n_s\cdot\mathrm{DOL}\), and the brightness per dye is \(e^{-\rho/\rho_q}\), a phenomenological self-quenching law under which brightness per volume \(\rho e^{-\rho/\rho_q}\) peaks at \(\rho = \rho_q\) and falls thereafter. In a whole mount, signal from depth \(z\) is attenuated by \(e^{-(\mu+\varepsilon\rho)z}\), with \(\mu\) the tissue attenuation coefficient and \(\varepsilon\) the dye's contribution; in a section both are zero. The secondary, at concentration \(C_s\), meets its own binding-site barrier against bound primary and reaches depth \(L_s = \sqrt{2Dt_sC_{s,\mathrm{eff}}/(B_T\theta n_s)}\), where \(C_{s,\mathrm{eff}} = C_s - n_s\cdot\mathrm{carry}\cdot C_0\) accounts for secondary sequestered by free primary carried over from an incomplete wash. The detected shell is the smaller of \(L\) and \(L_s\), and the integrated signal is the volume integral of \(\rho e^{-\rho/\rho_q}\) times the attenuation over that shell.

Consequences. With no crowding, quenching, attenuation or carryover, a fixed \(C_s\) caps \(L_s\) at a value independent of \(C_0\) once the rim is saturated, so the signal rises to a plateau and stays there: secondary limitation alone cannot produce a fall. Carryover drives \(C_{s,\mathrm{eff}}\) to zero at \(C_0 = C_s/(n_s\cdot\mathrm{carry})\) and the signal with it; because sequestration is stoichiometric the fall is steep, a threshold rather than a slope. Crowding and self-quenching reduce rim intensity as \(\theta \to 1\), so the rim dims while the interior may not. Attenuation leaves the rim bright and dims the interior in proportion to rim dye density. A directly labeled primary has signal proportional to \(B_T\theta L\) and is monotonic in \(C_0\) under all four. Test: monotonicity of the ideal, secondary-limited and direct-label curves, a cap from limitation, and a fall of at least 2 percent from the peak under carryover, crowding and quenching.

Boundaries. The shell model replaces the full diffusion–reaction profile with a saturated front; it is accurate when binding is fast relative to diffusion (Thiele modulus \(k_{\mathrm{on}}B_TR^2/D\) much greater than one) and \(C_0\) is well below \(B_T\). It ignores antigen internalization, cell-to-cell variation in antigen, interstitial fraction and tortuosity, and treats the secondary's diffusion coefficient as equal to the primary's.

The random section plane

A sphere of radius \(R\) is cut by a plane at offset \(z\), uniform on \((0,R)\) for sections that hit the sphere. The apparent radius is \(\sqrt{R^2-z^2}\), with mean \(\pi R/4\). A shell of true thickness \(t\) appears with thickness \(\sqrt{R^2-z^2} - \sqrt{(R-t)^2-z^2}\) when \(z < R-t\), and the section appears fully stained when \(z \ge R-t\), a fraction \(t/R\) of sections. The calculator integrates over \(z\) numerically. Test: the mean apparent radius against \(\pi R/4\), the fully stained fraction against \(t/R\), and the apparent thickness against a 200,000-sample Monte Carlo.

Spheroid size from Poisson seeding

Cells per drop are \(\mathrm{Poisson}(\bar n)\) with optional extra dispensing variance \(\mathrm{CV}_x^2\), so the count CV is \(\sqrt{1/\bar n + \mathrm{CV}_x^2}\). Radius scales as \(\text{count}^{1/3}\), so by the delta method \(\mathrm{CV}_R \approx \mathrm{CV}_{\text{count}}/3\). Test: closed form.

Numerics

All calculators are deterministic. Simulations appear only in tests, with a seeded mulberry32 generator. The hook calculator integrates over 200 radial shells; the Wicksell calculator over 2,000 section offsets (400 in the sweep); the bivariate normal over 400 trapezoid panels on \((-8, a]\).

Boundaries

The atlas evaluates idealized models. Parameters are the user's; none is estimated from data here. The entries are the well-documented processes with primary literature, not a census of all randomness in biology. Passing tests shows that the code implements the stated equations and is not biological validation. The implementation is original code written from the public literature cited in each entry; no employer-provided data, constructs, methods or internal results were used.
